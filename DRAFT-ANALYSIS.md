# Draft analysis

The top-level `#/draft` route opens Summary; `#/draft/2021` through `#/draft/2026` open each authoritative ESPN draft. `?pick=40` opens a filtered board for an insight's underlying selection. All leaderboards expand to every eligible row.

Generate offline with `python football_draft_analysis.py`. Full ESPN generation calls the same builder after metadata generation; Pages also rebuilds it from normalized snapshots. No new network source or credentials are required. Canonical analytical picks are stored once in `draft-analysis.json`; compact references supply rounds, insights, and leaderboards. React resolves references, filters tables, and formats Python-calculated metrics.

## Precise definitions

- **Season fantasy points:** sum the player's ESPN `appliedTotal` actual weekly league-scored points in `player-scoring.json` for weeks represented in that season's fantasy matchups (including fantasy postseason). Week 18 is excluded when the fantasy league does not use it. Counts the selected player's performance independent of fantasy ownership or active-slot starts. For incomplete seasons, use fully final fantasy weeks only, withholding all final ranks and performance metrics.
- **Coverage:** a week requires a finite score, explicit `played=false` in normalized NFL game records, or a schedule-proven bye for a single known historical NFL team with at least 16 schedule weeks. An unresolved total stays null. A real zero or negative score is valid. Missing metadata never establishes a zero. Conflicting source scores withhold season performance metrics. A season also requires its completion flag, a complete authoritative draft board, and all configured weeks in the scoring contract.
- **Final overall / positional ranks:** descending totals across all covered scored players, including undrafted players. Use average ranks for ties (1.5, 1.5, 3). These ranks describe the covered pool; they cannot account for unobserved players.
- **Primary draft value:** for each season/position's eligible drafted cohort, let N be its size, D the player's ordinal selection order within that cohort, and F its average-tie finish rank within that same cohort. Value = `100 * (D - F) / (N - 1)`. N <= 1 yields null. Positive is better. This yields a comparable -100 to +100 ordinal movement scale without comparing overall picks to positional ranks. Draft order and finish have the same coverage population. It measures realized order reversal, not VOR, ADP expectation, or absolute player quality; a player drafted first at a position cannot gain ordinal value. Read alongside final positional rank and finish percentile.
- **Positional gain:** `D - F`, the raw same-position cohort movement. **Finish percentile:** `100 * (N - F) / (N - 1)`. **Overall gain:** actual overall pick minus final overall fantasy-point rank, explicitly secondary because quarterback scoring affects it.
- **Within-round rank:** descending player totals, average ties. A round is withheld if any selection has unresolved scoring. **Normalized round score:** `100 * (round selection count - rank) / (round selection count - 1)` (denominator bounded at one). Every tied maximum wins; top-three includes ties at the third-highest selection's points.
- **Team aggregation:** average value per eligible pick determines primary order; total is contextual and gets its own volume-sensitive leaderboard. Eligible/drafted counts distinguish exclusions. High value is >= +25, success is > 0. Consistency is population standard deviation of season-average values, requiring >=2 evaluated seasons; lower can still mean consistently poor. Under ten picks or two seasons receives a small-sample label. Round rank and normalized score are complementary, not a composite.
- **Tendencies:** average round, earliest selection, normalized pick `1 + (overallPick - 1) / teamCount`, share of all manager picks, median and mean first-selection round. First-selection statistics use exactly one first pick per manager/position/season. Completed eligible seasons only; number of seasons and picks shown. No significance claim.
- **Rookies:** matched metadata `rookieSeason == fantasy season`; earlier known rookie year means non-rookie. Missing/future metadata is unknown. D/ST is not classified. NFL draft round/year does not establish rookie status, allowing undrafted rookies. For each rookie with known points, controls require the same season, position, and four-round draft band (1–4, 5–8, 9–12, 13–16), >=3 non-rookies with known points. Compare points to their mean. Report matched sample size; descriptive association, not causal inference. Unknown players are excluded from known-classification counts.

## Identity

`draft-manager-identities.json` explicitly enumerates memberships by season AND team ID, with an expected owner-name check. It records the stable owner names in the normalized snapshots rather than guessing from team ID, team branding, or team names. Review this file when owners or seasons change. Missing membership or an owner mismatch produces a season-specific identity and does not merge into an existing manager. Duplicate memberships fail generation. This is a curated mapping from the available owner evidence, not verification against private ESPN member IDs.

## Snapshot audit

2021–2025 are completed; 2026 is provisional. Completed boards have 896 picks total, with 853 eligible values. Unresolved totals by season: 2021: 5, 2022: 26, 2023: 3, 2024: 5, 2025: 4. All 14 historical owner identities match the explicit mapping. Player rookie metadata covers every drafted non-defense in the completed seasons. There are 83 matched rookie/control observations in this snapshot. These counts change on regeneration.

Conservative missing-data handling can exclude injured, traded, or otherwise sparsely observed players and entire round comparisons. This is exposed in the UI, not repaired with invented zero totals. All-time ordering can also reflect tenure, survivorship, draft strategy, and the ordinal cohort definition; individual statistics remain visible instead of hiding these choices in a composite. Total value intentionally has no league-size or pick-count fairness guarantee; compare normalized per-pick averages for that purpose.
